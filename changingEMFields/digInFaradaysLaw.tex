\section{Changing electromagnetic fields}

 Time-varying electric and magnetic fields are coupled by Maxwell's equations. Maxwell--Faraday's law relates the curl of $\vec{E}$ to $-\partial\vec{B}/\partial t$, while Ampere--Maxwell's law includes $\partial\vec{D}/\partial t$ as well as conduction current. These laws do not require both fields to be time-varying in every situation.



\subsection{Faraday's law}

Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction states that the electromotive force (voltage) induced in a loop is equal to the change of magnetic flux through the loop in time. The flux can be changed by physically moving the loop or changing the current through the loop. The minus in the equation below has to do with the polarity of induced voltage (and the direction of induced current)  that we will discuss in the next section on Lenz's law.

\begin{equation}
e=-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\end{equation}

For a stationary loop, the electromotive force is the closed line integral of the electric field. The contour direction and surface normal are related by the right-hand rule.

\begin{equation}
e=\oint_C \vec{E}_{ind} \cdot \vec{dl}
\end{equation}


Equating the above two equations for a stationary loop, we get Faraday's law of induction.


\begin{equation}
e=\oint_C \vec{E}_{ind} \cdot \vec{dl} =-\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
\end{equation}

For a moving material loop whose local velocity is $\vec{u}$, the total emf includes the magnetic force per unit charge: $e=\oint_{C(t)}(\vec{E}+\vec{u}\times\vec{B})\cdot d\vec{l}=-d\Phi/dt$, where $\Phi=\int_{S(t)}\vec{B}\cdot d\vec{S}$ is the flux through the moving loop. For a multi-turn coil, use flux linkage in place of single-turn flux.
