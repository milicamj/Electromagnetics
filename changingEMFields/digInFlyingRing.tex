\section{Flying Ring}

The flying-ring experiment is shown in Figure \ref{fig:FlyingRing}. An AC-driven coil on a ferromagnetic core induces a current in a nearby conducting ring. Lenz's law states that the ring's response opposes the change in linked flux, not necessarily the instantaneous coil current. The resulting average magnetic force can lift the ring if it exceeds the ring's weight.

In a linear sinusoidal model at a fixed ring position, choose current references such that the mutual inductance $M>0$. The ring circuit satisfies $(R_r+j\omega L_r)\tilde{I}_r=-j\omega M\tilde{I}_c$, so $\tilde{I}_r/\tilde{I}_c=-j\omega M/(R_r+j\omega L_r)$. Only in the high-$Q$ limit $\omega L_r\gg R_r$ does this ratio approach the negative real value $-M/L_r$, corresponding to currents nearly $180^\circ$ apart. An RL current lags its applied voltage by $\arctan(\omega L/R)$, approaching $90^\circ$ only when resistance is negligible. With finite ring resistance, the currents are not exactly opposite and the instantaneous force can vary in sign. For the usual geometry, with coupling decreasing as the ring rises, the average force is repulsive.
