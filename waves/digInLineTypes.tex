Any wire, cable, or line that guides energy from one point to another
is a transmission line. Whenever we make a circuit on a breadboard,
every wire attached forms a transmission line with the ground wire. Whether we see the propagation (transmission line) effects on the
line depends on the line length, and the frequency of the signals used. 
At lower frequencies or short line lengths, we do not
see any difference between the signal's phase at the generator and the load,
whereas we do at higher frequencies.

\section{Propagation modes on a transmission line}

Ideal coaxial and two-wire lines in a homogeneous dielectric
support TEM propagation. Microstrip generally supports
quasi-TEM propagation: its longitudinal field components
are small enough to neglect within an appropriate frequency
range. The accuracy of this approximation depends on the
line geometry, materials, and frequency; there is no universal
upper frequency limit. The approximate limits quoted here
are 30--40\,GHz for unshielded microstrip and 140\,GHz for
shielded microstrip; these require a supporting reference
and the corresponding design assumptions.

For propagation along the $z$ direction:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Transverse electromagnetic (TEM): $E_z=H_z=0$.
\item Transverse electric (TE): $E_z=0$, with $H_z\ne0$.
\item Transverse magnetic (TM): $H_z=0$, with $E_z\ne0$.
\end{enumerate}

The transmission-line model developed here applies to TEM
propagation and approximately to quasi-TEM propagation.
